Coach's Read: Is This Enough?

  • Protein is solid, not excessive. ~129g/day at ~65.8kg is roughly 2.0g/kg — a strong number for lean recomposition. It's well spread across 5 feedings, which matters more than the total.
  • Fibre is good but lopsided. The lunch sabzi and weekend salad carry most of it. Consider adding a small serving of vegetables to breakfast too (spinach already helps) so fibre isn't concentrated in one meal.
  • Carbs are on the low side for your training load. ~92g on a day with a 6:30am session plus an office day is lean. This is fine for fat loss, but on your two hardest training days (HIIT + Legs), an extra small portion of fruit or a banana post-workout would protect performance without derailing the deficit.
  • Micronutrients are well covered — B-complex, omega-3, and magnesium are the right stack for someone sitting at a desk most of the day and training early morning.
  • One gap: electrolytes. At 3.5–4L water/day plus sweat from training, plain water alone can dilute sodium. A pinch of salt in your pre-workout water, or an electrolyte sachet on training days, is worth adding.

Coach's Read: Program Corrections

  • Structure is sound — 5 days, no overlap. Back/biceps, chest/triceps, endurance, shoulders/abs, legs/core covers every major group with a clean pull-push-cardio-stability-power split.
  • One real gap: standalone rest. Five straight training days into Sunday recovery is workable short-term, but for 12 weeks, consider making one of Day 2 or Day 4 lighter every other week to avoid accumulating fatigue — your joints will thank you closer to week 8–10.
  • Badminton-specific footwork is under-trained. The program builds the muscles and the engine, but there's no direct lateral shuffle / split-step drilling. Adding 5 minutes of shadow footwork before Day 3's run would transfer more directly to court movement.
  • Progression isn't specified. Reps/sets are fixed at 3×10–12 throughout — for genuine recomposition over 12 weeks, plan to add small weight increments (2.5kg jumps) every 2–3 weeks once the top of the rep range feels easy, rather than staying static.
  • Running progression needs a target curve. "Increase running range in 2–3 months" needs a number — a simple plan is +0.5km on the sustained finisher every 2 weeks, so 1.5km in week 1 becomes a full 5km continuous by week 10–12.

Section 04

The Breakdown

Why the timing, the seated angles, and the fasting windows are doing what they're doing — in plain terms.

01The Weekday Fasting Window+

Consolidating meals between 6:15am and 5:00pm creates an overnight fast of over 13 hours — a mild version of early time-restricted feeding.

By around 10pm, circulating insulin drops to baseline. With insulin low, AMPK activates and unlocks stored fat for use as fuel.

Training at 6:30am, before the first meal, means glycogen is available but blood glucose is low — pushing the body toward fat oxidation to power the session.

02Muscle Protein Synthesis vs. Breakdown+

Staying lean without losing muscle means keeping MPS ahead of MPB — and the timing here is deliberate.

The Leucine Trigger: eggs + soy at breakfast deliver 3g+ of leucine, the amino acid that switches on the mTORC1 muscle-repair pathway.
The Casein Trickle: curd at lunch releases amino acids slowly over 4–5 hours, protecting muscle through the office stretch. Weekend paneer extends this overnight.

On soy: 40–50g of soy chunks daily does not measurably shift testosterone or estrogen — its isoflavones bind ERβ receptors, which don't produce the effects people worry about. It's simply an efficient, affordable protein source.

03Why Every Lift Is Seated or Supported+

The whole program chases mechanical tension in the target muscle while removing axial load and lumbar shear from the spine.

Seated rows vs. bent-over rows: standing rows force the lower back to brace against gravity — sitting and bracing the feet routes the load into the hips instead.
Bench press vs. standing overhead press: a bench absorbs the load directly; a standing press drives it straight through the lower back.
Goblet box squats vs. barbell squats: the front-loaded dumbbell keeps the torso upright, and the box gives a controlled depth marker so the lower back never rounds out.
04Building Two Different Engines+

A 5K and competitive badminton draw on two separate energy systems, and Day 3 is built to train both.

Badminton (ATP-PC / anaerobic): explosive bursts and direction changes. The 1-minute hard sprint intervals condition rapid, oxygen-independent energy output.
Marathon (aerobic / VO2 max): sustained oxygen efficiency and lactate clearance. The 1.5–2km continuous finisher builds the stamina base for both the run and long badminton rallies.
